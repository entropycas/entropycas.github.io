Soft Robotics

Through biomimetic design principles, I developed soft robotic structures using Rhino8 and Adafruit.

Rhino8 Soft Robotics Prototyping Adafruit

Research Overview

By studying how marine organisms move and adapt under Dr. Yin Yu's guidance, I designed and simulated soft robotic structures using Rhino8 and some physical prototypes. Using 3D print molds, silicone rubbers, and Adafruit microcontrollers, I simulated small-scale ambulatory movement. These ideas can be expanded for applications in underwater exploration, managing the nitrogen cycle, and adaptive structures.

Learning Rhino8

After familiarizing myself with Rhino8's interface and tools, I created various soft robotic designs inspired by natural organisms. I focused on creating flexible structures that could mimic the movement of soft-bodied creatures. A challenge I faced was making sure the models were both functional and manufacturable using silicone molding techniques.

Using Silicone Rubbers

After designing molds in Rhino8, the models were then put into Bambu Studio and printed in PLA to cast the silicone rubber components. This process involved careful consideration of mold design to ensure easy removal of the cured silicone parts. I experimented with different silicone formulations from Smooth-On to achieve the desired flexibility and durability for the robotic structures.

Adafruit Microcontroller

To bring the soft robot to life, I used the Adafruit CRIKIT and Playground Express microcontrollers. I programmed simple inflation and deflation patterns using CircuitPython, allowing the soft robot to perform basic locomotion tasks.
